# MD-IT-005 — Hardware Request & Replacement Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs requests for laptops, monitors, docks, keyboards, headsets,
printers, and other workplace equipment, including replacement of damaged or
failed devices. It is the source for tickets such as “my laptop screen is
cracked,” “battery will not charge,” “I need a second monitor,” and “the meeting
room camera is broken.” The policy distinguishes an ordinary equipment request
from a business-blocking failure, sets approval and return requirements, and
routes physical equipment work to Workplace IT. It does not authorize employees
to purchase unapproved equipment and seek reimbursement after the fact.

## 2. Scope

The policy applies to company-owned endpoint and shared-room hardware used by
employees and contractors in Meridian Dynamics offices or approved remote
locations. It covers standard equipment, loaners, warranty repair, accidental
damage, lifecycle replacement, and accessibility peripherals. Mobile phones
owned by employees are excluded except when their loss affects MFA, which
follows MD-IT-004. Network appliances, office Wi-Fi, and VPN services are owned
by IT Infrastructure. Software installation and subscriptions are governed by
MD-IT-006 even when software is bundled with a device. A lost or stolen company
device must be reported as a security incident under MD-IT-008 before a
replacement is processed.

## 3. Definitions

**Standard hardware** is an item on the current Workplace IT catalog and does
not require architecture review. A **replacement** exchanges an assigned item
that is failed, unsafe, lost, or beyond its supported lifecycle. A **loaner** is
a temporary managed device issued while repair or replacement is pending.
Employees commonly describe a hardware incident as “laptop dead,” “cracked
screen,” “dock not detecting monitors,” “keyboard broken,” or “printer jammed.”
A **business-blocking failure** prevents the employee from performing essential
work and has no usable alternate device. **Accidental damage** is an unplanned
physical event such as a drop or liquid spill; reporting it promptly does not by
itself imply employee fault. **Lifecycle replacement** is a planned exchange
initiated by Workplace IT based on support age and device health.

## 4. Policy / Procedure

1. The requester records the asset tag when available, equipment type, physical
   location, symptoms, number of affected people, and whether a safe alternate
   device exists. Photos may show damage but must not display confidential data.
2. Workplace IT checks remote diagnostics and completes safe basic steps. An
   employee must power off a device that is swollen, smoking, unusually hot, wet,
   or electrically unsafe and must not reconnect it to power.
3. A failed standard device under warranty is repaired or exchanged without
   manager approval. A net-new item, upgrade, second device, or nonstandard model
   requires manager approval and a stated business or accessibility need.
4. When a business-blocking failure cannot be repaired remotely within two
   hours, Workplace IT offers an available loaner. The replacement device is
   enrolled and encrypted before release.
5. The employee returns replaced hardware within five business days using the
   provided chain-of-custody instructions. Workplace IT records erasure or
   disposal. A lost or stolen device is not handled as ordinary damage and is
   escalated immediately under MD-IT-008.

## 5. Priority & escalation

Workplace IT owns `hardware` tickets. A planned monitor, peripheral, or lifecycle
request is low urgency, P4, with a one-business-day initial response. A single
employee with degraded hardware and a workable alternate is medium urgency, P3,
with a four-business-hour initial response. A single employee with no usable
device before a time-critical customer or operational event is high urgency, P2,
with a thirty-minute initial response and loaner evaluation. Failure of shared
meeting-room or printer equipment affecting 10 or more employees is P1 only when
it stops a critical business operation and no alternate room or device exists;
otherwise it is P2. Fire, smoke, battery swelling, suspected tampering, or a lost
device is escalated immediately to IT Security and Facilities as applicable.
MD-IT-002 controls any priority conflict.

## 6. Related documents

MD-IT-002 defines the impact and urgency matrix used for business-blocking and
multi-user equipment incidents. MD-IT-008 governs lost, stolen, or tampered
devices and evidence preservation. MD-IT-009 defines equipment issuance and
return during onboarding and offboarding. MD-IT-010 defines minimum standards
for equipment used from a remote location. MD-IT-006 governs software that may
be requested alongside hardware.

## 7. Revision history

Version 1.0 became effective on 2026-07-01. It introduces the five-business-day
return requirement, two-hour loaner evaluation threshold, explicit electrical
safety stop conditions, and distinct approval rules for standard replacement
versus net-new or nonstandard equipment.
